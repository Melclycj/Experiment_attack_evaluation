# R-G3-04 — A's rejected input or an originless/control callback gains a verified_subject at a consumer.

Re-run at follow-up step 4 (2026-10-19) on 04 version 7. Design line numbers are those of the earlier run
(si and conn E-09 / E-02 rows, re-read against the fourth-revision blobs si `c8aff1a37d73561b` and conn
`0c16e567a34aa441` where cited below); engine file:line refers to the prototype at commit 3ec67985 (engine code
equal to 74833c58).

## 1 What the adversary does
A's rejected input or an originless/control callback gains a verified_subject at a consumer.

## 2 How it would produce false attribution
- **Preconditions:** A is the adversary of execution X. It sends anything on fd 3 (A-01, A-02): a fragment
  that fails T-22 (ancillary) or T-23 (uid, sentinel, pid) and so produces no D-ACC; or a message that T-25
  ignores as stale or phase-wrong; or it drives lifecycle events — `bye` (T-48), EOF (T-46), exit of PID 1
  (T-42 → T-43 execution_empty, later uid_released) — or leaves a worker or connector callback with no live
  RequestEntry before release (after release the callback is T-51, G4, and R-GX-05's subject).
  Configuration: all three under the common abstraction.
- **Transitions traversed:** T-20 (a) → T-21 → T-22 or T-23 rejection (no D-ACC), or T-21 … T-24 → T-25
  drop; or T-46 / T-48 / T-42 / T-43 as lifecycle inputs; or T-34 with an originless callback → T-38. T-38
  is the only owned step that attaches a verified_subject to anything, so the route is T-38 attaching one
  to: (a) a receive_rejected event, filled from the Channel's row (the expected subject) or from the
  rejected fragment's observed uid; (b) a lifecycle event (session_closed, execution_empty, uid_released,
  spawn_*), retyping its control_binding — execution_id, lease_id, expected_uid — as a sender; (c) a
  callback event with no RequestEntry, with a subject synthesised from the handle, the ProviderLease or the
  pool. A T-25 drop of a stale or phase-wrong message is not this route: that message is a D-ACC event with
  an origin, and emitting the row's own label for it is not false attribution (02 §4.1 item 3); emitting a
  different subject for it would be R-G3-03.
- **Sufficient condition violated:** C-08, component p82 (D_82, true → R-G3-04): an event without a D-ACC
  origin gains a verified_subject, or lifecycle control_binding is retyped as a message sender. Why it is a
  wrong attribution: the engine's records, and any consumer reading them, would hold an attributed event
  for which no accepted byte and no sending task exist — the originless case of 02 §4.3 item (i). Every
  D-ACC event in the history may be correct (the history is in F_8); the defect is a verified subject
  without an origin.


## 3 What stops it
verified_subject exists only as a copy of the VerifiedContext that the receive entry produced for a validated
message; a receive_rejected event carries none and is never filled from the connection's expected subject, and
lifecycle events carry control_binding, a different field — realized in the prototype's recorder and checked by
test and code reading. The three design rules and their realization:

- **Two fields, two owners (T-38).** Design: startup and lifecycle events use the control correlation of
  execution and lease and claim no verified agent subject; control_binding is a field whose name does not say
  sender; verified_subject is copied only after full E-02 validation, absent fields are omitted, agent_report
  never enters it (si L59–60, L91, L93, L99, L106; EV-126, EV-113). Realization: VerifiedSubject and
  ControlBinding are distinct frozen types; VerifiedSubject is built only by from_context, called only on a
  received message's context (phases.py:91, requests.py:155); the recorder accepts the thirteen verified kinds
  only with a VerifiedSubject and no control binding, refuses a subject on every other kind, and requires a
  ControlBinding on the control kinds — session_closed, execution_empty, uid_released, the spawn_* kinds and
  the trusted caller's task_call_* kinds among them (records.py:28–35, :206–226; EV-225); lifecycle facts are
  recorded through record_lifecycle with ControlBinding.from_execution (records.py:286–294); absent groups are
  omitted (records.py:145); agent_report is a separate group of task_call_resolved. Tests: the recorder
  type guards latch on a subject with uid 0, the overflow uid or a sender uid that is not the comparison
  result (EV-224); every verified-kind record's subject is the row's copy and every other kind carries none
  (EV-183).
- **Rejects have no subject (T-22, T-23 as Step 1 facts; T-38).** Design: receive_rejected carries no
  verified_subject and does not echo the payload; E-02 acceptance gives no authorization and no subject-bearing
  provenance for missing, truncated, duplicate, sentinel or wrong-uid credentials; the sentinel rule holds on
  the record path (si L68; conn L529; mech L263–266; EV-127, EV-40). Realization: receive_step returns Reject
  before any VerifiedContext exists on a rejected read, a failed parse or a phase refusal, and its caller
  records receive_rejected with no subject and closes the row (EV-172); the C reader rejects pid 0, uid 0, the
  overflow uid, 0xFFFFFFFF and any uid other than the bound expected uid before a frame is formed (EV-171, its
  comparison clauses holding at commit 7b4ab727, EV-265); since 7b4ab727 a malformed body passes the C reader and
  is rejected by the Python parse, still recorded as receive_rejected with no subject (EV-272).
  Tests: a phase refusal builds no subject and rejection diagnostics carry no agent text (EV-224); a first
  fragment from another uid, a uid change inside one frame and another pool uid are rejected before a frame
  forms (EV-174); uid 0 and the overflow uid are rejected (EV-175); six synthetic missing, truncated,
  duplicate and sentinel credential buffers are rejected (EV-176); SCM_RIGHTS on a frame ends the row with
  receive_rejected (EV-181); the expected uid is write-once and the observed uid is never written to the row
  (EV-186, EV-187).
- **Originless callbacks release (T-34).** Design: a callback for an entry that is terminal, or whose session
  is no longer open, is release-only or the late-result event bound to the original request; workers supply
  bounded facts and cannot choose a session or write the log (conn L620, L656; si L72, L111–112; EV-122).
  Realization: handlers reach the request only through a Sink that holds its entry (EV-198) and deliver by a
  callback posted to the executor (EV-231); after a terminal a late chunk only releases its reservation and a
  late result adds only a late_completion record carrying the original entry's subject copy (requests.py:327–331,
  :364–400); a callback selected for a retired registration is not dispatched (EV-192). Tests: a stale sink
  after close reaches nothing new, a late result does not rewrite the terminal, a late reply does not overwrite
  (EV-223). A late event that carries a copy of the original request's subject has a D-ACC origin; val row 208
  (wording approved by the user 2026-10-19) states that it never carries a new or changed one.

A T-25 drop of a stale or phase-wrong message stays outside this route, as section 2 says. In the prototype the
phase rule runs before any VerifiedContext exists, so a phase-wrong message yields a Reject with no subject
(EV-172); a stale cancel or task_result is a message with a D-ACC origin, recorded as message_received with a
copy of its own context and otherwise answered only by a diagnostic (requests.py:528–534; tasks.py:356–375).

Preservation rows relied on: T-34, T-38 (this folder). T-22, T-23 and T-25 are cited as Step 1 facts; no G2
preservation conclusion is used.

## 5 Disposition
**supported under stated premises** — the same value as the `disposition:` field.
- Premises that must hold: the engine is honest (AA-04) and its code is the prototype's at commit 3ec67985, in
  which every event passes the recorder's type check (records.py:140–158); the receive-side sentinel rule holds
  at T-23, which is G2's to argue and which the prototype realizes (EV-171, EV-175); handlers are the
  prototype's stub handlers; the three configurations share the path (AA-06). This is a conclusion on the
  frozen design and its checked realization in the prototype, not a claim about the deployed system.
- Evidence limit (configuration, substrate, run dates): runs of 2026-10-19 (int
  20261019T011226Z-engine-pool-70000 and sbr 20261019T010923Z-sandbox-pool-70000, repeating the 2026-10-18 runs
  val names); at commit 7b4ab727 the C reader parses no body and the integration and
  sandbox reruns passed (EV-265, EV-270, EV-271); originless callbacks are checked for the stub handlers and are
  to be re-checked for each real connector; EV-176's credential buffers are synthetic; no E-09 export consumer
  exists, so the absence of a subject is checked at the recorder, not at any reader of the export. The arm3 row
  is context only. All three configurations alike at the design layer.
  Shared limits: [coverage-uncertainties § Shared evidence boundary](../../convergence/coverage-uncertainties.md#shared-evidence-boundary-every-route-not-repeated-below).

## 6 What remains
- Residual and open questions: the E-09 export goes to a directory agents do not mount, so a subject on an
  originless event would be a record defect rather than an authorization; its harm is to attribution records
  and to any later consumer of them (U-06). The schema question about the receive_rejected record is answered
  (CR-S4-10, user ruling 2026-10-20): the row's execution_id is removed from its reject group. Since commit
  9981dd7b the record carries connection_ref, source_ref and a reject group of the cause and the detail only,
  as si L68 lists, with no execution label and no verified_subject (EV-285); a test asserts the group's two keys
  and fails on the earlier code (EV-286). EV-258 records the earlier field. Diagnostic records (kind diagnostic) carry session_id in a
  detail group, never a subject; the same consumer caution applies. The after-release callback shape is
  R-GX-05's.
- Design obligations for the engine: (T-38) the event constructor refuses a verified_subject on every kind
  whose owner is not the validated-request path, and control_binding and verified_subject are distinct types —
  realized (EV-225, EV-224); (T-34) a callback without a live RequestEntry yields a release and no new subject —
  realized for the stub handlers (EV-223); (T-22 / T-23, G2's) the reject event is built without a subject —
  realized (EV-172), the reject group carrying no execution label since 9981dd7b (EV-285, EV-286).
- Cross-references: R-G3-01 (a fabricated accepted-subject event, C-01), R-G2-03 (truncated or missing
  ancillary treated as a usable sender on the compare path — G2's; this route is the record path), R-GX-05.
  Obligation #6 (reference-monitor correctness) is instantiated, the prototype evidence bearing on its record
  part; mech D4 / CRA-F15 is touched as the receive-side rule the emission inherits; C3 and C4 (kub §6) are G2's
  inputs and are not altered here; C1 and C2 are not touched.
