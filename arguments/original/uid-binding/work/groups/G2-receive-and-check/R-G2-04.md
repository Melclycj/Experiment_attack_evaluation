# R-G2-04 — A's current-frame bytes are mixed with another channel/frame's checked buffer and inherit its label.

## 1 What the adversary does
A's current-frame bytes are mixed with another channel/frame's checked buffer and inherit its label.

## 2 How it would produce false attribution
- **Preconditions:** two executions X and Y with their rows and channels; the adversary (X) times its
  bytes so that the engine's buffer for Y's frame absorbs them, or a buffer of an earlier frame on X's own
  channel is reused with a different TaskCall, or a readiness callback or fd number is re-addressed to the
  wrong buffer. Both allocations are current (not a released generation, which is q05 → R-GX-01).
  Configuration: all three branches under the common abstraction (agent ↔ engine leg).
- **Transitions traversed:** T-20 → T-21 → T-23 → T-24 on channel X, interleaved with the same on channel
  Y; T-26 and T-51 as facts only (callbacks hold their original objects).
- **Sufficient condition violated:** C-01 p13 (the witness belongs to the same read, channel, generation and
  frame as a) or C-05 p51 (the row read at a corresponds to the original Channel).
  B(R-G2-04) = D_13 ∧ ¬(q05 ∨ q06) ∧ q07. Why it is a wrong attribution: X's bytes parsed as part of Y's
  frame receive Y's label, so Exec(b) = X ≠ L(a).execution = Y.

## 3 What stops it
One FrameReader per Channel, emptied per frame, with the label read through that Channel's own objects
cuts the route at the buffer and at the label. Design text (conn at the fourth-revision blob
0c16e567a34aa441): E-02 (L448–450) — a Channel has exactly one receiver and one FrameReader, which holds the
original Session and generation, at most 4 header bytes and one bounded body, and no unbounded fragment
history; step 1 (L453–454) — each step reads the remaining length of the current header or body on that
channel's fd; step 7 (L475) — after a frame the FrameReader is emptied before the next header, with no
cross-frame prefetch; L477–479 — an old partial frame is never reused across Channel or TaskCall; E-02
VerifiedContext (L572, L579) — the label fields are the original engine objects and a response callback
holds the original RequestEntry and Channel and is never re-addressed when the same fd number or request id
appears later. The kernel keeps the two queues apart by socket object: T-04 creates one socketpair per
execution and T-09 installs it as one Channel (IG-12, by construction; IG-09).

Realization in the prototype (commit 3ec67985). A FrameReader is created with its Channel at install and
freed at close step 2; the C reader binds the fd, the socket cookie, the expected uid and the overflow uid
on first use, dies if any of them differs later, and resets after each whole frame (EV-236, read at
transport.c:212–239 of 3ec67985 on 2026-10-19; the reset is at L238 of 7b4ab727, which removed the C-side
body parse and changed nothing else in the reader, EV-265). Two frames in one send are taken one after the other (EV-238). The
label is copied from the Session and Channel the reader belongs to (EV-172, EV-186), the Channel → Session
link being made only at install with a new Channel object and cleared only at close step 2 (EV-194).
Callbacks act only while their Channel is current, and the loop dispatches a selected event only to the
registration current at selection (EV-192); with the same uid and fd number reused by a new row, old
callbacks, deadlines and late facts did not reach it (EV-190, EV-191), and a second execution on a reused
fd number was attributed to its own row (EV-189). Two executions run in parallel were each attributed to
their own row (EV-188). Preservation rows relied on: T-21, T-23, T-24.

**Composition:** conjunction of the per-Channel reader and label (EV-96 and EV-78 [S] realized by EV-236,
EV-172, EV-186 and EV-194 [R]; exercised by EV-238, EV-188 and EV-189 [M]), the object-bound callbacks
(EV-192 [R]; EV-190, EV-191 [M]) and the kernel's per-socket queues (T-04 construction; EV-94 [R]). The
cookie check does not lock the fd against replacement between syscalls (EV-91); the cut does not rest on
it but on exclusive ownership of the fd by one Channel and one reader, which EV-169 and EV-236 record. No
necessary item remains `[S]`; the result is not measured end to end.

**Proposed evidence (not yet in the registry):** an integration case with two channels whose partial
frames interleave with executor turns between fragments, recording that each frame is attributed to its
own row (EV-188 runs two executions in parallel but does not record interleaved partial frames). — not registered (2026-10-19): it names a run not yet made, so there is no observation to check.

## 5 Disposition
**supported under stated premises**
- Premises that must hold: the single executor (AA-01); the kernel at the pinned tag; configuration: all
  three branches on the agent ↔ engine leg. Design-layer and kernel-semantics conclusion with the
  prototype's checked realization; nothing here is a claim about the deployed system.
- Evidence limit: integration run 20261019T011226Z-engine-pool-70000 (2026-10-19) and the AA-13 run
  20261018T152246Z-aa13-deregistration (2026-10-18). Shared limits: [coverage-uncertainties § Shared evidence boundary](../../convergence/coverage-uncertainties.md#shared-evidence-boundary-every-route-not-repeated-below). The checks' own limits: fd-number reuse inside one
  round is made by the tests (low fds held, or os.dup2), not by the real spawner, which reuses numbers only in
  sequence (EV-189); the AA-13 check covers the epoll backend only.

## 6 What remains
- Residual and open questions: MM-15 — resolved in 01 v9 as one receiver_generation per Channel, set at
  install; the prototype makes it write-once (EV-187). The stale-endpoint and numeric-reuse variant across a
  close is R-GX-03 (G2 + G4), and the association remainder is R-GX-06. The C-01 remainder is R-G2-11.
- Design obligations surfaced for the engine: keep the FrameReader emptied before the next header and never
  handed to another Channel or TaskCall; keep readers and callbacks registered by object, not by fd number,
  when another event-library backend is used.
- Cross-references: R-GX-01 (prior-generation buffer), R-GX-03, R-GX-06, R-G2-11. Obligations #2 and #6.
